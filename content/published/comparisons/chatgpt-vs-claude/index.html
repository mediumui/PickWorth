ChatGPT vs Claude

ChatGPT or Claude — which should I pick?
Match the work: everyday mixed work and multimodal input → ChatGPT. Long-form, precise instruction-following, or sensitive material → Claude.
Which is better at coding?
Claude holds the edge on long-form coding and refactors; ChatGPT's ecosystem and the free tier make it the better everyday tool.
Which has the bigger context window?
Claude's long-context tier is the differentiator; ChatGPT's varies by model tier and is the limit where Claude still wins.
Which is cheaper?
Both start at the $20/month tier. Claude's top tier costs more; ChatGPT's free tier is the more useful one.
What is PickWorth's call?
ChatGPT: the wider product. Claude: the stronger long-form choice. Score the two per dimension, not by benchmark alone.
Do I need both?
For most people, one is enough. Add a second only when a specific kind of work starts hurting — see the $20 rule.

Where ChatGPT wins

On everyday speed and on the sheer breadth of what you can do without leaving the window, ChatGPT still leads. Its multimodal input — voice plus image in one conversation — is the most complete in the consumer set, and its integration story is deeper: a wider tool set and more add-ons. If your work is a mix of quick drafts, image-understanding and the occasional coding task, it is the better all-rounder.

Where Claude wins

On long-form work and on instruction-following, Claude is the stronger tool. The context window is larger and holds structure better, which is the reason many developers migrate specifically to stop hitting the ceiling. And on the agentic side — Claude Code and Claude Cowork — it is the most capable in-ecosystem coding and task-delegation set on the market. If your dominant task is a long document or a strict multi-step constraint, this is the one to pick.

The cost question

At the entry point the two are tied at $20 a month. They diverge at the top tier: ChatGPT's $200 Pro tier is a single step, while Claude splits its upper range across a $100 and a $200 Max. For a heavy daily user the Claude Max is the more flexible option, but you pay more for it. The honest answer is to stay on Pro on either side until it genuinely bottlenecks you, and only then move up.

Running both is a legitimate strategy

A lot of professional users keep one for everyday work and the other for the long-form task that justifies the second subscription. The cost of doing that is roughly $40 a month — less than one day of a consultant, and less than the cost of using the wrong tool on the wrong task.
