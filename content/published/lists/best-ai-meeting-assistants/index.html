Best AI meeting assistants in 2026

Every one of these records, transcribes and summarises your calls. The difference is what sits on top of the transcript: Fireflies adds a conversation-intelligence layer built to feed a CRM, Otter bets on the accuracy of live captions, and Fathom gives away the most. Pick on language needs and team size first, then on whether you need the notes to flow into a sales workflow.

The job, split three ways

The category is not one tool. A note-taker joins your calendar meetings and transcribes them. A capture tool records on your device, with or without a bot. A CRM layer adds the question layer — talk-time, sentiment, topics — on top of the transcript. Asking which is best without naming the job produces a ranking that fits nobody.

The two names everyone searches are Fireflies and Otter, and the honest split between them is the whole decision: Fireflies treats a meeting as a data source feeding a CRM record, Otter treats it as text to be captured accurately and read later. Both ship free plans that let you judge transcript accuracy on your own meetings.

Fireflies: the team default

Fireflies is the integration-heavy tool: it joins Zoom, Google Meet and Teams calls, transcribes in 100+ languages, and routes the output into Salesforce, HubSpot, Zapier and Slack with little setup. On top of the transcript it adds a conversation-intelligence layer — talk-time ratio, sentiment, topic detection — that turns a stack of calls into something a manager can review in minutes. The free plan is the more permissive of the two majors: unlimited transcription with 400 minutes of storage per team and a limited pool of AI credits. Paid Pro starts at $10 a seat a month on annual billing; Business at $19 adds multi-language mode and team analytics. The trade: the free tier caps AI summaries, and the auto-joining bot can feel intrusive in a small, informal call where nobody expects a visible recorder.

Otter: live captions and value

Otter is built around the accuracy of its live, real-time transcription: captions appear in-call on web and mobile, with word-level speaker highlighting. That makes it the pick when you read along with a call or want the cleanest spoken-word capture. The free Basic plan gives 300 transcription minutes a month but caps every individual meeting at 30 minutes — that shows up fast in longer workshops. Pro runs $8.33 a user a month on annual billing (1,200 minutes a month, 90 minutes per meeting) and Business $19.99, with unlimited meeting length and concurrent calls. It is the value play for someone recording four or five short English calls a week who wants a searchable transcript and nothing more.

Fathom: the free plan that gives away the most

Fathom is the recurring recommendation when budget is the deciding factor: it records unlimited calls with no time cap on the free tier, which the two majors do not match. It is the right starting point for anyone new to AI meeting notes — start free, and only pay when the team features and a searchable archive matter. The honest framing is that it is the floor of the category, not the ceiling: if you will pay, Fireflies or Otter usually earns the seat.

Notta: multilingual, with a warning

Notta is the multilingual workhorse — real-time transcription in 58 languages plus translation across 42 — and its free plan (120 minutes a month) is a fair trial. But treat the trial as a hard gate: a large share of its public reviews are one star, with consistent complaints about cancellation and weaker accuracy on non-native English. If your calls are mostly English, Otter is the cleaner choice; pick Notta only when language spread is the actual reason you are buying.

The picks, by scenario

  • Team notes that feed a CRM: Fireflies — unlimited transcription on every plan and the broadest integration list.
  • Individual, mostly English, live captions: Otter — cheapest Pro and the most accurate real-time captions.
  • Trying the category for free: Fathom — unlimited recording, no time cap.
  • Multilingual calls across 50+ languages: Notta — but run a free trial first.
