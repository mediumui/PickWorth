The short version

Name the pile before you buy. If four or more hours a week of meetings are currently being noted by hand — or not at all — the meeting tools earn a seat: start free on Fathom for unlimited recording, and pay for Granola Business at $14 a user only if the no-bot capture matters for client work. If the pile is documents, lecture slides and research you read but rarely revisit, NotebookLM is the free default — a source-grounded answer engine that costs nothing to start. Two jobs, not one app

Most of the names in this search are one of two things. Meeting-first tools — Granola, Otter, Fathom — join or capture a call and hand back a clean, editable summary with speaker labels and action items. Knowledge-first tools — NotebookLM, Notion, Obsidian, Mem — let you ask questions in plain English and get answers pulled from your own documents, with sources. A few try to do both, but each is clearly stronger at one job, and the price points are different enough that mixing them up wastes a subscription.

Granola: capture without the bot

Granola's signature is the bot-free capture: it records from your device audio rather than joining the call as a visible robot, which is the privacy and courtesy advantage for client-facing work. The summaries it produces are editable, it remembers meeting history so you can search or ask questions across past calls, and it ships pre-meeting briefs and follow-up emails on paid tiers. The free Basic plan caps meeting history at 30 days; Business at $14 a user a month removes the cap and adds advanced integrations and MCP access. It is the pick when the no-bot behaviour is the whole reason you are buying.

NotebookLM: the free knowledge default

NotebookLM is the most underrated entry in the category and it is free: upload lecture slides, PDFs, web links or your own notes, and it answers only from your sources with citations — no hallucinated facts pulled from the open internet. The free tier includes 100 notebooks, 50 sources each, and 50 chats a day with no card. It is the knowledge-tool default for students and researchers, and the reason a dedicated $15-a-month knowledge app rarely earns its price for solo users.

Otter: live transcription and value

Otter is the value pick for live transcription: the most accurate real-time captions in the meeting-tools group, with speaker identification and a searchable transcript. The free plan is 300 minutes a month, capped at 30 minutes per meeting. Pro runs $8.33 a user a month on annual billing (1,200 minutes, 90 minutes per meeting) and Business $19.99 with unlimited meeting length and concurrent calls. It is the cleaner tool for someone who records short English calls and wants a transcript to skim, not a CRM layer.

Fathom: the free tier that gives the most

Fathom is the recurring recommendation when budget is the deciding factor: unlimited recording with no time cap on the free plan, which Granola, Otter and the others do not match. Start free on it; pay for a seat only when the team features and a searchable archive actually matter. It is the floor of the meeting category, not the ceiling.

Notion AI: the one to skip

Notion AI is the complete workspace — docs, databases, and an AI that searches and writes across it — but it is not a standalone note-taking tool you should buy for the notes. Its full AI layer requires the $20-a-user Business plan, so for a solo user chasing AI note-taking it is not competitive with free NotebookLM or a $12-a-month capture app. It earns the price only when the team is already running on Notion and AI is a feature of the workspace rather than the reason for the subscription.

The picks, by scenario

  • Four+ hours a week in calls: start free on Fathom, pay for Granola Business only if bot-free capture matters.
  • Reading PDFs, slides and research: NotebookLM — free and source-grounded.
  • Short English calls, live captions: Otter — cheapest Pro, most accurate real-time transcription.
  • Already on Notion as a team: add Notion AI — the workspace, not just the notes.
