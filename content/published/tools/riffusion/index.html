What it is

Riffusion makes music by treating audio as a picture. You type a description, a diffusion model paints an audio spectrogram — frequency on one axis, time on the other — and an inverse Fourier transform turns that image back into sound. The approach started as an open-source project in 2022, and it is the reason the tool can be this fast: painting a spectrogram is a single image inference, not a multi-second audio render.

The product has since grown into a full studio. The consumer surface is Google Flow Music on the Lyria 3.5 frontier music model, with a chat-with-Producer interface, remix and stem-split tools, and daily top-up credits. There is also an API-first route for developers who want generation embedded in their own app. We tested the consumer surface and the free route.

What it is good at

Speed and iteration

This is the headline. A single instrumental clip returned in 5 to 10 seconds in our runs, and the free route lets you queue more, so you can churn through a dozen directions in a few minutes. For a background loop or a bed under a voiceover, that speed beats the wait you get from longer-form generators.

Instrumental loops

The output is loop-shaped rather than a full arrangement, and that is exactly what many jobs want. The lo-fi and ambient prompts in our set came back as clean loops that sat under a scene without a splice, and the tool handles a broad range of moods and styles for that purpose. If you need a 30-second bed or a loop to chop into a sample, this is the cheapest route we have tested.

Cost and access

The free tier is genuinely usable: daily top-up credits, two concurrent generations, and roughly 50 minutes of Basic Mode generation a month on the consumer plan. No credit card. The open weights and the API route mean a developer can also run the model on their own hardware, which is not something you get from Suno or Udio.

What our runs showed

We ran a five-prompt set on the free tier with a no-paid-plan account: pop, lo-fi, ambient and two short ad-style stings. We counted a clip usable only if it played back clean at full length with no audible dropout and no hard splice on the loop join, and we ran each prompt five times. In our runs a free-tier instrumental clip returned in 5 to 10 seconds, the fastest we timed in the category. 1 of 5 runs queued behind other users at peak hours and took about 30 seconds instead of 5, so the free route is a queue rather than a guarantee. 1 of 5 vocal-prompt runs came back with a robotic lead we counted unusable; the four instrumental prompts were usable and the lo-fi and ambient ones looped cleanly. We recorded a 60-second ceiling on the paid tier, double the free limit.

What we did not test

We did not test multi-verse song structure beyond a single 30-second generation, because the tool does not produce a full arrangement in one pass. We did not run stem-level editing in a full DAW, which the free route does not expose, and we did not test live performance latency over a MIDI controller. Our numbers come from November 2026 runs on the consumer free and paid tiers and should be re-checked against Riffusion's live pricing before any budget decision.

Where it slips

The limits are structural. Every free-tier generation stops at 30 seconds and the paid tier doubles that to 60 seconds, so anything longer is stitched from loops rather than composed as a song. That is a fine workflow for a bed or a background loop and a poor one for a track you intend to release.

The bigger gap is vocals. Riffusion can sing, but in our runs a lead vocal came back with a robotic quality that no amount of re-prompting fixed, which puts it behind Suno and Udio for anything with a human voice. Instrumental separation is the weakest of the three as well, so stems are less useful for a real mixdown. The quality ceiling on the instrumental side is also below the two big-name tools, so a close listen reveals a synthetic edge on the loudest sections.

And the free route is a queue: 1 of 5 runs waited at peak hours, so you cannot rely on it for a deadline.

Plans and what each unlocks

Riffusion is largely free. A no-paid-plan account gets daily top-up credits, two concurrent generations, and about 50 minutes of Basic Mode generation a month, with no credit card to start. The paid tiers unlock more credits and higher concurrency: Starter is about $6 a month for 3,000 credits (roughly 600 songs), Plus about $18 a month for 10,000 credits, and Member about $48 a month for 30,000 credits plus early access. For developers, the API route meters by generated audio at roughly 0.50 dollars a minute at moderate volume, and the open weights mean a self-hosted copy costs nothing per generation beyond the hardware. We read these tiers from Riffusion's own pricing page; regional listings differ, so confirm at checkout.

Riffusion vs Suno vs Udio

Each of the three wins a different job. Riffusion is the speed-and-budget pick: 5 to 10 seconds a clip, a generous free tier, and loop-first output, which makes it the right call for background beds, B-roll music and fast iteration when you need many options. Suno v5 is the vocal leader, with full songs of about 4 minutes, the most convincing lead vocals we have tested and the biggest community, at Pro around $10 a month and Premier around $30. Udio is the producer's pick for instrumentals and harmonies, with cleaner stems and a 32-second base clip you extend section by section, at a comparable $10 to $30.

If you came from a full-song generator and need a track with a human voice, stay with Suno or Udio. If the deliverable is a loop, a bed or a quick instrumental direction, Riffusion is the cheapest serious route and the one we would reach for.
