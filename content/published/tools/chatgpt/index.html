ChatGPT review

The short version

ChatGPT is the most broadly useful general-purpose AI assistant on the consumer market, and it is not close. It leads on multimodal input, on integration depth with the wider OpenAI ecosystem, and on everyday writing. It lags on long-document reasoning, where Claude has the edge, and peak-hour usage caps on the free tier are the most consistent complaint across the user base we read.

What it is

ChatGPT is OpenAI's general-purpose assistant: chat, writing, coding help, file analysis and image understanding in one interface, available as a web app, a mobile app and an API. It is the most-used AI product in the world — the Wikipedia entry for ChatGPT records 900 million weekly active users by February 2026 and the service ranking as the fifth-most visited website globally as of September 2026.

Under the hood it runs on OpenAI's GPT model family. The consumer product wraps the model with a conversation layer, a tool system (search, image generation, file reading) and a memory layer that can carry context across conversations. The distinction matters because it means the assistant is as good as the model generation underneath it, and those generations move fast — the "GPT-4" that was state-of-the-art in 2023 is the entry-level experience by 2026.

What it is good at

Everyday writing and quick drafts

For a first draft of an email, a meeting summary or a short report, ChatGPT is the fastest tool available. Users consistently report that the value is not in producing a finished piece but in collapsing the blank-page stage to about ninety seconds. That is a real workflow gain and it is the reason the free tier is good enough for most people who only use it a few times a day.

Multimodal input

ChatGPT accepts text, audio and image input in one conversation, and its image-understanding is among the best in class. A photograph of a broken part, a screenshot of an error, or a whiteboard sketch all work as starting points in a way that still surprises people who first met it as a text box.

Coding and debugging

On a single file or a short module, ChatGPT is excellent. On a whole codebase — the kind of task that now dominates the professional use of these tools — it is weaker than the dedicated coding agents, and that is a category problem more than a capability one.

Where it slips

Long-document reasoning

ChatGPT degrades noticeably as the input document grows longer. Developers report that past a certain context length — commonly cited around 30 to 40 thousand tokens in our reading of the community — it starts to miss details that are present in the material. If your workflow is a 200-page document, Claude's longer-context models are the stronger choice, and that is a real gap, not a preference.

Peak-hour usage

The most consistent complaint across the user threads we read is that the free tier, and the lower tiers of the paid service, hit usage caps on weekdays during working hours in popular time zones. The caps are not uniformly applied and the messaging around them has shifted several times, so we treat them as the one part of this review to re-check before you buy.

Confident wrong answers

The failure mode that has not gone away: a plausible-sounding answer to a factual question that is simply wrong, delivered without any visible uncertainty. Every other assistant makes this error; the difference is in how often, and in how well the tool flags that a claim is uncertain.

What it actually costs you

The sticker price and the real cost are different. For a light user the free tier is enough. For a professional who runs the assistant for several hours a day, expect to land on Plus and spend a couple of dollars of model usage on top of the subscription — enough to feel it, not enough to change the math.

The hidden cost is the integration. If your workflow already sits in the OpenAI ecosystem (the developer API, the mobile apps, the enterprise offering), ChatGPT is the natural fit. If you are building on a different provider's model, the tooling on this side is less convenient, and the honest question becomes "is the convenience worth the lock-in."

Our testing notes

We tested ChatGPT on the Plus tier over a two-week window using three recurring workloads: a first-draft email task, a 40-page document summary, and a small JavaScript refactor. The first task took about a minute. The second task hit the long-document limit we described above — the summary was usable but missed two substantive sections. The refactor worked well within a single file but the agent-style handoff across files was more limited than the dedicated coding tools.

Verdict

ChatGPT is still the default for most people, and that is not just because of brand. It is the fastest path from a blank screen to a usable first draft, its multimodal input is the most complete in the consumer set, and the free tier is genuinely useful. The reasons to look elsewhere are specific: you need a longer context window than it handles well, or you want the assistant to sit on top of a non-OpenAI model. Neither reason is about it being bad — both are about fit.

Before you pay

Check your usage caps first. The free tier will tell you in a week whether you would be paying for a limit that actually bites, and the answer is often no. The $20 per month is not the question; the question is whether the extra capacity would change your actual workflow.
