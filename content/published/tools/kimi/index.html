Kimi review: the $0 plan for long documents

Reviewed 26 October 2026 Updated 26 October 2026 PickWorth Editorial
3.9Our score

The short version

Kimi is the assistant to reach for when the job is measured in documents, not sentences. Its million-token window and free flagship model make it the cheapest way to analyse a whole book, a 200-page filing, or a legacy codebase in one pass, and the open weights mean you can self-host when data cannot leave your walls. The deductions are real but specific: English creative prose trails the US frontier, the free tier is metered rather than unlimited, and the consumer app's prompt-training policy closes it off to confidential work. Use it if your week is built on long documents, Chinese-language work, or cheap agentic research. Keep a Western assistant for voice, polish, and the larger tooling ecosystem. Score 3.9 of 5.

The short answer. Kimi is Moonshot AI's long-context assistant that puts a near-frontier, open-weight model on the free plan and a million-token window on paid tiers, and it is the right pick when your work is measured in whole documents rather than quick chat.

What is Kimi?
Kimi is the consumer assistant and developer platform from Moonshot AI, a Beijing lab founded in 2023. It is built around a very long context window: where most Western assistants cap a conversation at a few hundred thousand tokens, Kimi holds entire books, codebases, and legal filings in one window. The current flagship is K3.1 Pro, a 3-trillion-parameter open-weight model with a one-million-token context, reached through the web and mobile apps, the Kimi Work and Kimi Code surfaces, or the pay-as-you-go API.
How much is Kimi free?
The free plan costs $0 and includes K3.1 Pro at limited usage, basic agent credits, and 500MB of project storage. The catch is metering: every message, chat and agent run draws from one unpublished credit pool that paid plans refill each month, so free is usable for real research but not for all-day heavy use. The million-token extra-long chat is not on the free tier; it is reserved for the Max and Ultra plans.
What is Kimi good at?
Three things. The context window is the headline: independent testers load 300- to 400-page documents and get cited, cross-referenced answers the US frontier assistants refuse or truncate. The open-weight K2 and K3 families let you self-host and keep data local. And the API is cheap, with K3.1 Pro at $2.00 per million input tokens and $10.00 per million output tokens internationally, roughly a third below the prior K3 rates.
Where does Kimi fall short?
English creative prose trails ChatGPT and Claude, and English answers occasionally carry Chinese idiom that reads like a translation. The free tier is metered rather than unlimited, and long-context inference runs slower per token. The consumer app trains on prompts with no opt-out on personal plans, which rules it out for confidential material, and the third-party ecosystem of guides and integrations is smaller than OpenAI's or Anthropic's.
How much does Kimi cost?
Membership is freemium, billed annually at effective monthly rates: Free $0, Plus $15, Pro $31, Max $79, Ultra $159. Max and Ultra unlock the one-million-token extra-long chat. The API is pay-as-you-go: K3.1 Pro at $2.00 input and $10.00 output per million tokens, the Highspeed variant at $4.00 and $20.00, and K3 at $3.00 and $15.00. The older K2 series is being retired for new users at the end of October 2026 and taken offline in December, so build new work on K3 or K3.1 Pro. Numbers are from Moonshot's official pricing pages, checked 24 October 2026, and they move.
What do hands-on users actually report?
The independent hands-on reports split along the product's design. The consistent praise is whole-document and whole-repository recall no Western assistant matches at this price, plus a free tier that genuinely does research work. The consistent friction is the credit pool running down faster than expected on agent and research tasks, English answers that read like translations, slow long-context responses, and hesitation before pasting confidential material into the consumer app. People keep Kimi for the documents it can hold and a US assistant for the prose.
Is Kimi K2 open weights, and does that matter?
Yes. The K2 generation that built Kimi's reputation in 2025 was a roughly one-trillion-parameter open-weights mixture-of-experts model, and the K3 family shipped open weights too, published on Hugging Face under a permissive licence. That matters because any organisation with the hardware can run the model locally and keep data off a third-party API, which is the route regulated or privacy-bound teams actually take. At 2.8 to 3 trillion parameters, self-hosting is realistic only for serious GPU capacity.

What Kimi is

Kimi is the consumer assistant and developer platform from Moonshot AI, a Beijing lab founded in 2023. It earned its name on one capability: context length. Where most Western assistants cap a single conversation at a few hundred thousand tokens, Kimi was built to hold entire books, codebases, and legal filings in one window, and that is still the reason most people open it.

The product has moved fast. The K2 generation that built Kimi's reputation in 2025 was a roughly one-trillion-parameter open-weights mixture-of-experts model strong at coding and tool use. K3 arrived in mid-2026 at 2.8 to 3 trillion parameters with a one-million-token window and open weights, and K3.1 Pro followed in October 2026 as the current flagship, keeping the same window while pushing benchmark scores above several closed models on coding and reasoning. You interact through the Kimi web and mobile apps, the Kimi Work and Kimi Code surfaces, or the pay-as-you-go API.

Where Kimi leads

The context window

This is the only reason the product matters against ChatGPT and Claude, and it is a real one. Independent testers load 300- to 400-page documents and ask for cross-reference work that the US frontier assistants refuse or truncate. Kimi returns cited answers with the kind of recall that built its reputation, and it does not lose the early pages the way a 128K model does. For analysts, lawyers, and researchers working across large source sets, coverage beats the deeper but shorter answers the competition gives.

A near-frontier model on the free plan

Moonshot puts its flagship on the free tier at limited usage rather than gating it behind a paywall. Free users get K3.1 Pro, not a deliberately weakened model, which is a different bet from OpenAI's approach of reserving the best models for paid plans. The catch is metering: every message draws from a shared, unpublished credit pool that paid plans refill each month. You are not buying a worse model for free; you are buying a finite allowance of the real one.

Open weights and a cheap API

The K2 and K3 families are open-weights, so anyone with the hardware can self-host and keep data local. On the API, K3.1 Pro runs $2.00 per million input tokens and $10.00 per million output tokens internationally, roughly a third below the previous K3 rates, with cheaper K2-class models still available for high-volume coding. For teams that need long context without sending documents to a third party, that combination is hard to match from a US vendor at this price.

File, PDF, and agentic work

Kimi reads PDFs, Word, Excel, and images with citations, runs web search with sources, and ships agent modes that build slide decks, spreadsheets, and simple websites from a prompt. The Swarm mode splits large jobs across parallel sub-agents, and Goal mode keeps a persistent plan across sessions. These are the features that make it a workspace rather than a chat box, and they are the reason it competes with Claude's and ChatGPT's agent surfaces rather than just with their chat.

Where it slips

English creative quality trails the frontier

Multiple independent reviews place Kimi's English writing and nuanced reasoning a notch below ChatGPT and Claude, especially on open-ended creative tasks. English answers occasionally carry Chinese idiom or phrasing, which reads as a translation rather than native prose. If your output is judged on voice and polish, that gap is visible, and it is the single most common complaint from hands-on users outside China.

Free limits and rate metering

The free plan is usable but not generous in the way unlimited chat sounds. Usage is metered against an unpublished credit pool, and long-context inference runs slower per token than shorter-context models. Heavy agent or research use on a paid plan draws from one shared pool that can run down and block other features until it refreshes, so the cost is not per-message predictable the way a flat subscription implies.

Privacy and data residency

Moonshot is a Chinese company, and its consumer app trains on prompts with no opt-out on personal plans, according to its policy. For confidential or regulated material, that rules the free app out for some buyers; Kimi Business and self-hosting exist for the no-training guarantee, but they are separate purchases. Western procurement teams also flag origin on policy grounds, which is a harder limit than any model score.

A smaller ecosystem

There are fewer prompt-engineering guides, community examples, and third-party integrations than for OpenAI or Anthropic. The web app is polished but region-locked features can surprise users outside China, and the desktop app needs Apple silicon with some modes still in beta. The tooling gap is narrowing, not closed, and it shows when you try to drop Kimi into an existing workflow built around the US assistants.

Plans and pricing

Kimi uses a freemium membership plus a pay-as-you-go API. Numbers below are from Moonshot's official pricing pages, checked 24 October 2026; they move, so confirm before you commit. Membership is billed annually at the effective monthly rates shown.

Pro $31 K3.1 Pro Highspeed, Goal mode, 2x agent credits

The one-million-token extra-long chat is reserved for Max and Ultra, which is the plan tier that actually unlocks the headline window inside the chat app. On the API, K3.1 Pro is $2.00 input and $10.00 output per million tokens; the Highspeed variant doubles those. K3 sits at $3.00 input and $15.00 output. The older K2 series is being retired for new users at the end of October 2026 and taken offline in December, so build new work on K3 or K3.1 Pro rather than on a model Moonshot is about to shut down.

What hands-on users actually report

The independent hands-on reports split cleanly along the product's design. The praise is consistent: whole-document and whole-repository recall that no Western assistant matches at this price, and a free tier that genuinely does real research work without a credit card. The friction is just as consistent: the credit pool runs down faster than expected on agent and research tasks, English answers sometimes read like translations, and long-context responses feel slow compared with shorter-window models. Privacy-minded users report hesitating before pasting confidential material into the consumer app, and several note the surprise of region-locked features. The pattern is clear: people keep Kimi for the documents it can hold, and they keep a US assistant for the prose and the ecosystem.
