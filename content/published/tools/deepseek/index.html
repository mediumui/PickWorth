What it is

DeepSeek is a family of open-weights large language models built by a Hangzhou research lab and backed by a quantitative-trading firm. The models are open-weight — the parameters are published and can be self-hosted — but the training data is not openly licensed. Since early 2025 the company has released its models under free, open-source licences, which is the single fact that changed the pricing of the whole category: anyone can run a frontier-class model locally or on their own hardware.

The consumer face is a free chat product. The serious product is the API, which is what this review prices. Two models matter: Flash, the default workhorse built for high-volume chat, extraction, classification and agent loops, and Pro, the frontier tier for the hardest reasoning and coding calls. Both carry a one-million-token context window and support thinking mode.

What it is good at

Cost per task

This is the whole reason the model exists. Because it is open-weights and the API is priced aggressively, a task that would cost several dollars on a closed frontier model can cost a few cents on DeepSeek. For high-volume production work — classification, extraction, summarisation, agentic loops that fire thousands of calls a day — the math is the reason to route through it at all.

Reasoning on the cheap

The reasoning capability that made R1 famous in 2025 has carried forward. A task that needs a think-first response can be run on Flash for a fraction of the closed-model price, and the quality on everyday reasoning is comfortably inside the frontier band.

Self-hosting and sovereignty

Because the weights are open, a team that needs data to stay on its own hardware has a realistic option that the closed APIs cannot offer. That is a capability, not a quality claim, but for regulated or security-sensitive workloads it is the deciding factor.

Where it slips

The free tier is a trial

The consumer chat product is generous for a week of exploration and then metered. For a real workload the free tier is not a plan; the API is where the product actually lives, and that is pay-per-token.

Peak and off-peak pricing is a trap

Since mid-2026 the API uses peak / off-peak billing, with peak hours around $0.30 per million cache-miss input on Flash and double the off-peak rate. A workload that lands in peak hours pays more for the same tokens. If your traffic is concentrated in US daytime, the off-peak headline numbers understate your actual bill.

Tool ecosystem is thinner

For a raw model it is excellent. For a consumer assistant with a deep tool and integration story it is narrower than the closed products. The value is in the model, not in the product layer around it.

Cache-hit input is roughly a tenth of the cache-miss rate, so a stable system prompt pushes the effective input cost down sharply. The exact figures move — treat these as the order of magnitude.

Verdict

DeepSeek is the price floor of the category, and it earns that floor. For high-volume production work, route the bulk of calls through Flash and keep Pro for the minority that genuinely needs it; the three-to-four-times spread between the two is the entire bill-management strategy. If you need self-hosting, the open weights are the only option that is also frontier-capable. If your traffic is concentrated in peak hours, budget accordingly — the off-peak headline is not your number. The one thing it is not is a closed-product experience, and nobody buys it for that.

Before you wire it in

Run your real workload through the Flash model for a week and log the token count. Then do the bill twice — once at off-peak and once at peak rates — and build your budget on the peak number. The difference is the margin you get to make or lose on the model.
