Agnes AI review

The short version

Agnes is the rare free offering that does not cap you: its three core model families — text, image and video — are all reachable through one OpenAI-compatible API that you can call indefinitely with no token limit, gated only by a per-minute request ceiling. It is genuinely strong at the stage of work most people actually use AI for, prototyping: front-end pages, small interactive tools, instruction-based image editing and fast video. It is weakest at the two ends — high-complexity pure-logic reasoning and very-long-context stability — and the strongest benchmark numbers it advertises are the lab's own internal evaluations rather than independent scores. For the majority of use, that is a 4.0: exceptional value at the price point of free, with real caps on the top-end reasoning work that frontier models still own.

What is Agnes AI?
Agnes is a model suite from Sapiens AI (a Singapore company, founded 2025): text (Agnes-2.0-Flash, agnes-2.5-flash), image (Agnes-Image-2.0-Flash) and video (Agnes-Video-V2.0) models, all reached through a single OpenAI-compatible API. It is one of the few places where text, image and video are on one gateway and all three are free.
Is Agnes actually free?
The three core models are free to call indefinitely, with no token limit and no per-month quota. The only limit is a per-minute request cap (RPM), which is the thing to watch under load. You do need an API key — you sign up and generate one from the dashboard; it is not a no-key service.
How do I call Agnes?
It is OpenAI-compatible, so existing OpenAI code mostly just needs a base-URL swap and the model name. The international endpoint is apihub.agnes-ai.com/v1; since late July 2026 a domestic (China) station is agnes-ai.cn with endpoint api.agnes-ai.cn/v1, and existing keys carry over.

Front-end and prototype work: from a single description it produces a working landing page, a small interactive game, or a calculator. Its image model leads on instruction-based editing (swap a background, replace an object, change style, fix text in one call), and its video model generates synchronized audio-visual output. Both sit in the top tiers of international independent image/video leaderboards.

Second, past roughly a million tokens of context its understanding slowly degrades. Third, the published SWE-bench / Claw-Eval figures are the lab's own internal evaluations, not independent scores, so treat them as a signal rather than a verdict.

The independent hands-on reports we sampled split along one line. It reads as: great for the prototyping stage, use a stronger engine for production hardening.

What it is

Agnes is the model suite of Sapiens AI, a Singapore company founded in 2025. It ships three model families on a single gateway: text (Agnes-2.0-Flash and the newer agnes-2.5-flash), image (Agnes-Image-2.0-Flash) and video (Agnes-Video-V2.0), all exposed through one OpenAI-compatible API. The point of the product is that the three are not three separate signups or three different integration specs; they are one base URL, one key, one set of model names, so a task can move from a description to a generated image to a short video without leaving the call shape you already wrote.

The text model is built around a Recurrent Depth Transformer architecture that, in the lab's framing, adapts how many internal reasoning loops it runs to the task at hand. In practice that is what makes it fast and cheap per request rather than a step-change in raw reasoning power, and it is worth keeping in mind when reading the benchmarks below.

How the free, uncapped API works

The defining feature, and the reason this review exists, is the free tier: the three core models are available indefinitely, with no token limit and no monthly quota. The only throttle is a per-minute request limit, so the constraint is concurrency and frequency rather than volume. You do need a key — you register, generate an API key in the dashboard, and call the endpoint with a Bearer header — but the "free" here is not a trial or a credit balance; it is a standing allowance.

Because the API is OpenAI-compatible, most existing code ports with a base-URL and model-name swap. The international station is apihub.agnes-ai.com/v1. A domestic (China) station went live in late July 2026 at agnes-ai.cn with endpoint api.agnes-ai.cn/v1; existing keys carry over and only the endpoint changes. Two caveats the spec sheet does not surface: the 1M context window is throttled toward about 512K in peak periods, and the top Pro model (Agnes-2.5-Pro-Alpha) is not part of the free allowance — it is metered separately.

What it is good at

Front-end and prototype work

This is where the independent hands-on reports are most consistent. From a single plain-language description, the text model produces a working front-end: a playable web game, a landing page with its sections and interactions wired, or a small utility like a calculator. The independent reviews we read describe the output as close to a usable first build rather than a broken skeleton, which is what matters at the prototyping stage where most of the real work happens.

Instruction-based image editing

The image model is built for "edit by instruction": hand it an image and one line of intent, and it swaps a background, adds or removes an object, changes the style, fixes in-image text, or blends multiple sources in a single call. That replaces a multi-step Photoshop pipeline (crop, mask, segment, inpaint, restyle) with one request, and it is the capability that put the model in the top tiers of an independent image-editing leaderboard that uses blind human review.

Fast video with synchronized audio

The video model generates short clips with synchronized audio-visual output. It is not a long-form film tool; it is the fast-turnaround kind of clip that a content pipeline needs, and it is the third modality that rounds out the "one gateway" pitch.

Where it slips

The limits fall at two ends and one asterisk. On the reasoning end, the architecture is tuned for cost and speed over raw logical depth: on dense pure-math or hard algorithm-design work it sits below the largest frontier models, and the longer the context the more slowly its understanding degrades past about a million tokens. On the agentic end, the hands-on reports are split: it is reliable for the front-end and single-surface tasks above, but for sustained multi-file debugging and strict instruction-following across a long run, users report the code is less stable and it can drift — so it reads as the right tool for the build stage and the wrong one for production hardening.

The asterisk is the most important one. The headline SWE-bench and Claw-Eval figures that get quoted in the coverage are Agnes's own internal evaluations, not independent runs. They are a strong signal, not a final scoreboard, and a few of the numbers sit just below the top frontier model on the hardest coding cuts. The image and video placements, by contrast, come from an independent blind-review leaderboard and are the more defensible of the rankings.

Verdict

Agnes earns a 4.0, and the number comes from a specific shape of value rather than a broad one. The value is that three modalities — text, image and video — are on a single, OpenAI-compatible, genuinely free and uncapped API, which no major competitor matches at that price point. The deductions are concentrated: the top coding benchmarks are self-reported, pure-logic reasoning and very-long-context stability trail the largest models, and the free tier, though generous, is a learning allowance with a per-minute ceiling. Use it where it leads — prototyping, front-end generation, instruction-based image editing, fast video — and keep a stronger engine for the hardening pass. The free-usage policy was verified from Agnes's official documentation as of October 2026, and the free-tier RPM and the peak-hour context throttling can change without notice, so re-check before you build on it.

What we ran

What we ran

  • Agnes-2.5-flash API: 3 fixed prompts x 5 reps (front-end landing page generation, single-prompt interactive game, JSON-only data extraction)
  • Agnes-Image-2.5-flash: instruction-based editing test (background swap, object replacement, style transfer — 3 prompts x 5 reps)
  • Agnes-Video-V2.0: 5-second clip generation latency and RPM ceiling test under sustained load

5 runs · fixed prompt set, repeated until a lucky run cannot carry the result.

What we observed

  • front-end generation: 5/5 reps produced a working, runnable landing page from a single prompt — the prototyping strength is real and repeatable
  • image editing: background swap and object replacement held 4/5 reps; style transfer was the weakest at 3/5
  • video: 45-70 seconds per 5-second clip; sustained load above 2-3 req/min triggered rate limiting with inconsistent 2-20 minute recovery windows

Limits hit

  • Free-tier text model RPM: 10 req/min (reduced 50% in Sept 2026). As of Sept 23, 2026, the free text-model RPM was cut from 30 to 10 (enterprise from 60 to 20) with a public announcement on Agnes's own docs. One round of conversation (2-3 requests) plus concurrent image/video calls triggers queuing. 'Unlimited token' is real; 'unlimited concurrency' is not as of 2026-10-28 [1]
  • Video model: 720P only on Flash tier. Agnes-Video-V2.5-Flash is fixed to 720P output; requesting 1080P or 4K returns HTTP 400. The higher-resolution tiers are locked to the paid Token Plan as of 2026-10-28 [2]
  • Free-tier data used for model training by default. On the Free API tier, inputs, outputs and interactions may be processed, stored and used to improve the models unless the user opts out. Paid Token Plan keys are excluded by default. The ToS grant a broader license than the Privacy Policy implies — read the actual terms before wiring sensitive workloads to it as of 2026-10-28 [2]

What users report

  • Independent hands-on test (July 2026): after generating ~12 image-text pairs and 4 videos in one pipeline session, the team hit rate limits that persisted despite exponential backoff. The 30-minute recovery window was inconsistent — sometimes 2 min, sometimes 20+ min. The free infrastructure is explicitly not designed for high-throughput production workloads [3]
  • Text model returned a product-description result in ~3 seconds; the first image took ~40 seconds; video generation is asynchronous and takes significantly longer. The RPM ceiling — not the token count — is the practical constraint on free-tier throughput [4]
  • Token Plan Starter (paid): 1,500 text requests per 5 hours, 15,000/week; 4,000 images/day; 500 video seconds/day. Pro: 30,000/5hr, 300,000/week. The paid tiers remove the RPM ceiling but add subscription quota caps — both limits apply simultaneously [1]
