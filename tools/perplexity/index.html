What it is

Perplexity is a web search engine that answers questions in prose and shows the sources it used to do it. It was founded in August 2022 by four engineers and launched its main search product in December of that year. As of the September 2025 valuation mark, the company had raised $500 million in a single round and was valued at $14 billion — a figure the Wikipedia entry cites as of that date, with a later reported figure around $20 billion.

The product is a search box that returns a synthesized answer rather than a list of links. That is the whole pitch, and it is the reason it is a different category from the competing chat assistants: Perplexity starts from the web and the answer is a second step, not the first.

What it is good at

Research that needs a source

For any question where the answer has to be traceable — a specification, a statistic, a recent announcement — the citation layer is the actual product. The competing assistants give you an answer you then have to verify; Perplexity gives you the answer and the source in one step. For professional research work that is the single most consistent reason people keep it open.

Freshness

Because it is built on live web search, the answer reflects the current state of the web rather than a training cutoff. For anything that changes — a product launch, a price, a policy — that is a real advantage over the assistants whose models were trained on an older corpus.

A focused answer, not a chat

Where the assistants are open-ended, Perplexity is task-shaped: a question in, a sourced answer out. That makes it better for the research loop than for a long-running working session, and that is a feature, not a flaw.

Where it slips

The free tier is metered

The most capable models are credit-metered and the free version runs out on a working day of real use. If you use it the way a researcher would, the free tier is a trial, not a plan, and Pro is the entry point for it to be useful.

It is not a general assistant

Perplexity is a search product, not a working partner. It does not maintain a long conversation the way the assistants do, and the tool ecosystem around it is thinner. If you want one window for everything, this is the tool you open for the research step and close after.

Source quality varies

The answer is only as good as the sources it pulls. On a well-covered topic the citations are solid; on a thin topic the synthesized answer can be under-sourced in a way that is easy to miss. The fix is the same as with any search product: read the links it gives you.

The legal backdrop

The Wikipedia entry for Perplexity records that the company has faced copyright and trademark scrutiny from several major media organizations, and that separate analyses by Wired and Cloudflare found it using web crawlers with altered identifiers to fetch content from sites that block scraping. That is a background fact, not a quality claim about the answers, but it is worth knowing: the source layer it sells has a non-trivial legal position, and a team evaluating it for sensitive work should read the terms.

Verdict

Perplexity is the tool to keep open for the research step of any project. The citation layer is the real value, the freshness is the reason it is not redundant, and the free tier is a trial you will outgrow. Pay for it when you are doing research daily; if you only need it a few times a month, the free version plus the free tiers of the assistants is enough, and Pro is overpaying.

Before you pay

Use the free tier for a week of real research questions. If the credit ceiling is hitting you mid-question, Pro is the answer; if the free tier is enough for your volume, there is no case for paying.
