What it is

Nano Banana is Google's image model, formally Gemini 2.5 Flash Image, and the name the internet gave it the week it shipped. It lives inside the Gemini app, Google AI Studio, and Vertex AI, so you can talk to it in a normal chat, wire it into an app through the Gemini API, or run it as an enterprise endpoint. What makes it different from the image generators that came before is that it edits by conversation: you upload a photo and say 'make it night' or 'put this product in that scene', and it changes the pixels while keeping the subject recognizable. That, plus unusually accurate text rendering and a free tier, is why it became the default recommendation for anyone who needs images with words on them or a quick edit of their own photo.

In 2026 the family has grown. The original Nano Banana (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image) is still the one developers call through the API and the one Google priced at $30 per million output tokens, about $0.039 an image. The model most people now meet in the Gemini app is Nano Banana 2, which third parties identify as Gemini 3.1 Flash Image and which launched in February 2026: same Flash speed, near-Pro quality, 4K support, and the ability to use up to 14 reference images for style consistency. Nano Banana Pro is the paid, higher-precision variant, native 4K, the best text and photorealism, and it sits behind the paid Google AI plans rather than the free experience. Judge the product you can use today on Nano Banana 2; treat Pro as the upgrade.

Where it leads

Text, logos, and layouts

This is the gap that matters most for practical work. Where older image models hallucinated letters, Nano Banana renders product labels, menus, invitations, and posters with the words spelled right and placed where you asked. In our runs it was exact on every text string we threw at it, including curved surfaces and small print. If your image has copy on it, and most marketing images do, this alone is worth more than a prettier default style.

Editing your own photo by talking

You do not learn a syntax. You upload a picture and tell it what to change, and it edits the last image in the thread, so a multi-step retouch, remove the person, then make it dusk, then add a lamp, happens in one place. That is a different job from Midjourney's generate-a-grid workflow, and for casual editing it is the easier of the two.

Photorealism and world knowledge

Because it rides on Gemini's understanding of the real world, it handles materials, lighting, and spatial sense better than models that only know aesthetics. Side-by-side tests with DALL·E, Midjourney, and FLUX consistently rank Nano Banana's photorealism first for people, products, and scenes.

It is free to start

The Gemini app gives every Google account a daily allowance of image generations at no cost, and the API is pay-as-you-go rather than credit-gated. That removes the 'should I even generate this?' friction that a $10 minimum, Midjourney, or a credit counter puts in the way.

Where it slips

The free cap is real and moves

Google's current help page lists up to about 20 images a day on the Basic plan with Nano Banana 2, but the company is explicit that the limit is dynamic, distributed across the day, and resets daily, not a hard promise you can plan production around. Older articles still quote 100 a day for the older model, which is why search results contradict each other. At peak demand Google has throttled the free Pro path down to a couple of images a day.

Base model vs Pro

Free generation runs on the base Nano Banana 2 model. It is good; it is not the photorealistic precision of Nano Banana Pro, which is locked behind paid Google AI plans as a 'redo' path. If you need client-grade 4K output, the free tier will not get you there.

Artistic style is not its lane

For mood, texture, and a distinctive authored look, Midjourney still leads. Nano Banana follows instructions; it does not impose a signature style. If the brief is 'make it feel like a film still', that is Midjourney's job, not this one's.

Crowded briefs and strict filters

We noted it can lose a count or a position in a busy prompt, asked for three tulips it drew four, and Google's content filters are stricter than most competitors, which can block legitimate edits.

Nano Banana vs Midjourney

The short version: they are complements, not rivals. Nano Banana wins on text, photo editing, photorealism, speed, and free access; Midjourney wins on artistic style, mood, and community. Nano Banana returns one image per request in seconds; Midjourney returns a grid of four to choose from and rewards learning its controls. Nano Banana has a public API; Midjourney does not. Pick Nano Banana when the image has words, is a real photo you are editing, or needs to be free, pick Midjourney when the image is judged on how it looks, not what it says.

Plans and what each unlocks

For consumers, Nano Banana is part of Gemini, not a separate product. The free Basic plan includes daily image generation on Nano Banana 2 with dynamic caps; Google AI Plus, Pro, and Ultra raise the daily count and add Nano Banana Pro 'redo' actions, roughly 50, 100, and 1,000 per day at the top. For developers, the Gemini API prices image output at $30 per million tokens, with each image costing about 1,290 output tokens, roughly $0.039 per image on the 2.5 model, and the current 2026 image-preview models on the API carry no free tier, so programmatic generation is pay-as-you-go from the first image.

Verdict

Nano Banana is the image model to reach for first in 2026 because it is free to start, exact on text, and edits your own photos by conversation, the three things that actually block most people from using AI images. It is not the artistic choice; Midjourney still owns mood and style, and the free tier's daily cap is dynamic enough that you cannot treat it as a production pipeline. But for product shots, real-photo edits, anything with words, and anyone who does not want to pay to find out, it is the one to beat, and the API pricing is cheap enough that scaling up is a line item, not a commitment.
