Midjourney vs DALL-E

Updated 29 October 2026 PickWorth Editorial
4.5Midjourney score

Who wins

These two are not competing for the same image. Midjourney is the better artist, its output has more of the look that makes a person stop scrolling, and on a hero image or a piece of concept art that gap is the reason to pay for it. DALL-E is the more precise executor, it follows the spec literally, and it is the one that gets the text in the image right, which is the task Midjourney still fails on the first pass. The cost story is straightforward: if you already pay for the assistant that runs DALL-E, the image is free on top of it, and Midjourney is a separate line. The honest answer is the one the comparison reviewers all land on, pick by the image, not by the leaderboard.

The short answer. Pick by the one job that matters in your image: Midjourney when the image has to look striking, DALL-E when it has to be precise. The gap is widest on text in the image and on literal prompt adherence, where DALL-E wins cleanly, and on pure aesthetic quality, where Midjourney still leads.

Midjourney or DALL-E, which should I pick?
Midjourney for the image that has to look good, DALL-E for the image that has to be right. A lot of people run both, and the cost of that is less than one hour of your own time.
Which one renders text in the image better?
DALL-E, decisively. Legible, accurate text on a sign, a logo or a poster is the one test where the difference is obvious and repeatable, and Midjourney still garbles it on the first pass.
Which one is more artistic?
Midjourney. The lighting, composition and detail on a concept or a hero image run further than DALL-E, which is clean and accurate but reads as a step less striking.
Which is cheaper?
For most people, DALL-E, because it is bundled into the $20 assistant they already use. Midjourney is a separate subscription from about $10 a month. The per-image API cost is also much lower on DALL-E.
Which one follows my prompt more literally?
DALL-E. Describe a precise scene and it does the scene; Midjourney interprets it, which is why it is both the reason it is better at art and the reason it misses a specific spec.
Can I use both for commercial work?
Yes, on paid plans, and the honest caveat applies to both: an AI-generated image is not copyrightable, so you get the right to use it, not the right to own it.

No. Reviews are written by a single editorial team, and plan details are verified against each vendor's own pages. Nothing on this page is sponsored.

Where Midjourney wins

On the image that has to be striking, Midjourney leads, and it is not a close call. The lighting, composition and detail on a hero image, a piece of concept art or a brand visual run further than DALL-E reaches, and that is the reason a working designer justifies the separate subscription. It is also the stronger tool for a series, because it keeps a character or a style consistent across many images, which is the exact job DALL-E flails on, the same prompt returning four different looks. For anyone whose output is the visual itself, that combination of quality and consistency is worth the cost.

Where DALL-E wins

On the image that has to be right, DALL-E wins. The single clearest test is text, where it renders a sign, a logo or a poster caption legibly and accurately on the first try, and Midjourney still garbles it. It is the more precise executor, describe a specific scene and it does the scene rather than interpreting it, which is also what makes it the safer tool for a spec that has to match a brief. And the convenience is real: it runs inside the assistant you are already in, you iterate on it in plain conversation, and you do not open a second app or learn a parameter syntax. For a small business that needs a product mockup with the right words on it, that is the whole argument.

The cost question

The cost difference is less about price and more about where the tool already is. Midjourney is a separate subscription, starting around $10 a month for the basic tier and rising to around $30 for the unlimited relaxed tier. DALL-E is bundled into the assistant's $20 plan, so for most people the image is not a new line on the invoice, it is already paid for. On the API the gap is wider, DALL-E runs a few cents per image, so the developer building a feature that generates images is the one who feels the difference. The one shared caveat both carry, and neither discloses in marketing: an AI-generated image is not copyrightable, so both give you the right to use the output commercially and not the right to own it.

Run both, and they cost less than the difference they make

For anyone who makes images as part of the job, the two are complementary layers, not competitors. Use DALL-E for the fast, literal, text-carrying work and Midjourney for the final image that has to look like a person made it. The combined bill is trivial against the time it saves, and it is the setup the working designers describe settling into.
